# 13 — Viewing and managing a campaign

**Who this is for:** the Account Manager for the client (and Traffic, an Account Director or the
Power Admin, who can do everything the account manager can). Department leads, team leaders and
team members read the same page; the leads act on what is theirs, and a team member only reads.

**What you need before you start:** a campaign that has been approved (guide 2 or 12). The example
throughout is **LUMA Season 2**, created with four phases in guide 12 — a few days in: the
Animation delivered, the Digital Banner in review, the TVC's task list still waiting on its owner.

---

## Finding a campaign

**Campaigns** shows every live campaign as a card. **Mine** (the default) is the ones you are the
account manager on; **All** is everyone's. Beside it, the **search** finds a campaign by anything
inside it — its name, the client, a piece's name or code, a person on the work — and the filters
narrow by client, person and status. **Show closed** brings finished campaigns back.

*(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: The campaigns list, searched for LUMA Season 2)*

Each card says, in one line, where the campaign is:

- **the client, the phase it is in** (*Pre-Launch · 1 of 4*) and the account manager;
- **progress** — how many pieces are *delivered*, of all of them;
- **the status**, and a badge for each thing that needs somebody, shown only when there is one:
  **at risk** (pieces whose remaining work will not fit before they are due), **waiting on client**,
  **tasks unassigned**, and **task lists to approve**.

*(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: One card, close)*

Nobody needs to remember a campaign's name: a piece's code is enough.

*(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: Searching FM-002 — the campaign that holds the TVC)*

**Open** goes to the campaign. The **⋯** menu holds the actions that are not the card's main one —
**Close out** on an approved campaign (it asks first, and says what it would call off).

*(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: The ⋯ menu)*

## The campaign page

At the top: the name, the client, the phase it is in, how many pieces, workstreams and people, who
made it and when, and the **account manager** (the pencil changes them — only somebody who could
approve the campaign may). Below, six **views** of the same campaign, and **Manage phases** on the
right.

### Grid — the sheet the campaign is run from

The Grid opens first.

- **Task lists to approve** — the pieces whose owner has not yet agreed their task list, by
  workstream, each saying who it waits on. **11 tasks** unfolds the list. An owner sees
  **Approve all** and a tick per piece here; everyone else sees who it waits on.
- **The sheet** — one row per piece, grouped by workstream: its code, name and format, the
  **owner** (Creative), the workstream's owner (Managed), everyone holding a task on it
  (Executed), the due date, its **status** and whether its task list is approved. **Edit** and
  **Open** on each row.
- **The filters** — workstream, phase, owner, status, and **Needs approval**. Each narrows the sheet.
- **Workstreams**, under the sheet — rename a group, give it an owner, move a piece between groups,
  or add a new one.

*(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: The Grid — one task list waiting, seven pieces, three workstreams)*

*(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: The Grid narrowed to Pre-Launch)*

A piece's status is never set by hand: it moves with its tasks — not started, in progress, in
review, **approved** (every task approved, inside the agency) and **delivered** (pressed by the
account manager once the client has it).

### The piece, opened

**Open** on any row — or a square, card or bar in the other views — opens the piece down the right:
its code, status and due date, **who owns it**, and **the whole story** — every task, with who has
it, where it is and when it is due — then the brief lines it is made from.

*(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: The Digital Banner — one task done, one in review, one in progress, three waiting)*

**Edit** opens the same dialog as the row; **Open in Deliverables** goes to the piece's own page
(files, inputs, the review).

### Edit — and moving a piece to another phase

**Edit** holds the piece's name, type, **phase**, due date, owner and quantity; its status is shown
as the five stages and cannot be set.

- **Phase** — choose another and **Save changes**: the piece moves. Only the account manager (and
  those who stand in for them) may move one.
- **Due** — after approval the date is the promise to the client; pressing it opens the date
  change, which asks for the client's agreement (guide 9).
- **Owner** — a new owner in another department moves the piece to their department, and the
  dialog says so before you save (ADR-0102).
- **Close this deliverable** — it will not be made; it asks first and stays on the record.

*(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: The Carousel moved from Week 1 to Week 2)*

A notice confirms every save; close it to carry on.

## Managing phases

**Manage phases** opens the list: rename a phase, change its start or end (the tick saves the
row), add one at the bottom, or delete one.

*(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: Manage phases)*

A phase **holding pieces cannot be deleted** — the notice says how many; move them first. An empty
one goes at once, and the phases after it are renumbered.

*(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: Deleting Pre-Launch is refused — it holds two pieces)*

**Turn phases off** is refused while more than one phase holds pieces — the notice lists how many
sit where. Move everything into one phase first. On a campaign without phases, **Turn phases on**
takes the same place and creates the first phase.

*(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: Turning phases off is refused while four phases hold work)*

## Adding to the campaign

### A piece, on the Grid

**+ Add a piece** on the Grid. Choose the **deliverable** from the catalogue (type to search), a
label if its name is not enough, how many, the **owner**, the **phase** and the **due date** — the
date is required, and one past the phase's end warns and is recorded.

*(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: Adding a Campaign Key Visual to Week 1)*

The owner's department is where it belongs; the catalogue entry lays down its task list at once,
waiting for the owner to approve it. The new owner is told; nobody else is.

## The other views

### Where is everything

One row per piece in a phase — it opens on the phase today falls in: **where it is**, **with
whom** (the owner and their department), for how long, **the one thing that moves it next**, the
due date and **the clock** — on time, at risk, or late. Late comes first, in red.

*(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: Where is everything — Pre-Launch)*

### Season map

Workstreams down, phases across, **one square per piece**, coloured by where it is; the phase
today falls in is marked. A row going grey to the right is work not started yet; a red square is
late. Press a square to open the piece.

*(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: Season map — the Animation delivered, the Digital Banner in review)*

### Board

Every piece as a card in the column of its stage: **Unassigned**, Not started, In progress,
**Waiting**, In review, Approved, Delivered. Nothing is dragged — the cards move as the work does.
Unassigned and Waiting are the two that need somebody today.

*(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: Board)*

### Timeline

One bar per piece across its working days, two weeks at a time — Friday and Saturday shaded, today
marked. A bar past its due date turns red; a **hollow** one has nobody on it yet. **Previous week**
and **Next week** move the window.

*(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: Timeline)*

### Brief — reading it, editing it, proposing more

The campaign brief itself, beside a rail: **how much of it is written**, **what it produced** (the
season map's squares, by workstream) and **which fields are empty**.

*(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: Brief)*

**Edit the brief** turns it into fields; **Save** writes it back. Only the fields you change
change.

*(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: Editing the brief in place)*

**Propose more deliverables** reads the catalogue against the brief again and proposes only what
the campaign does not already have. Name each row's owner, phase and due date, then **Add** —
existing pieces are never touched, and each new owner is told.

*(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: Proposing more — a Story, due after Week 1 ends, warned and allowed)*

## The campaign elsewhere

- **Pipeline** — pick the campaign: **phases down, departments across**, each cell that
  department's pieces in that phase with their status and due day; each phase says how many of its
  pieces are signed off. A phase filter at the top narrows it to one row.

  *(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: The Pipeline for LUMA Season 2)*

- **Schedule → Project** — pick the campaign and a **phase**, and the grid shows the people booked
  on that phase's work. The filters sit in the address, so the view can be shared.

  *(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: The schedule, LUMA Season 2, Pre-Launch)*

## What a team member sees

The same page and every view: the task lists and who they wait on, every piece, the drawer.
**Open** on every row and nothing else — no Edit, no + Add a piece, no Manage phases, no ⋯ menu on
the list. Anything the page does not offer, the database refuses too, in words, if it is tried
another way.

*(Screenshot withheld from the public copy: The Grid, as a team member)*

## The rules

- **Who may shape the campaign** — add pieces, move them between phases, manage the phases, edit
  the brief, propose more: the campaign's account manager, or Traffic, an Account Director or a
  Power Admin.
- **Each owner approves their own pieces' task lists** (ADR-0100). Nothing under a piece can be
  assigned before.
- **A piece's department is its owner's** (ADR-0102); a workstream only groups (ADR-0099).
- **Status is read from the tasks**, never set. *Approved* is the agency's; *Delivered* is pressed
  by hand once the client has it.
- **At risk and late are measured from the work** — the hours still to do against the time left
  (ADR-0075).
- **A phase holding pieces cannot be deleted; phases cannot be turned off while several hold
  work** (ADR-0096). Move the pieces first.
- **Every piece has a due date.** One cannot be added without, and after approval it moves only
  with the client's agreement.
- **A closed campaign is read-only** until it is reopened (guide 10).

## What happens next

A piece added or moved keeps its tasks and its owner. A new piece's owner is told, and approves its
task list on the Grid before its work can be assigned (guide 5), then it is assigned on the
schedule (guide 6). Every view reads the campaign as it is now.
