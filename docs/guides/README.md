# Maestro — how-to guides

One guide per flow, written for the person who does it. Each one has the same shape:

1. **Who this is for** and what must already exist before you start.
2. **The steps**, numbered, with a screenshot wherever the screen matters.
3. **The rules** — what Maestro will warn you about, what it refuses, and what it records.
4. **What happens next** — who is told, and where the work goes.

The screenshots come from a demonstration workspace ("Northwind Beverages", "Dev Account Mgr" and
so on). Your screen shows your own clients and colleagues, but the layout and the buttons are the
same. Times are shown in Iraq time throughout.

## The vocabulary, once

- A **campaign** is a piece of client work with more than one deliverable, possibly across several
  departments. A **single job** is one request, one deliverable, one department.
- A campaign may run in **phases** — numbered, named, dated slices (pre-launch, week 1, week 2…).
  Every deliverable on such a campaign belongs to one phase.
- A **deliverable** is the thing the client receives — a key visual, a set of captions. It has an
  **owner** (accountable for it) and a **promised date**.
- A deliverable is made of **tasks**. Each task has exactly one **assignee** (who does it) and at
  least one **reviewer** (who approves it).
- **Tasks are approved. Deliverables are signed off.** When the last task under a deliverable is
  approved, the deliverable signs itself off — nobody has to do it separately.
- The **account manager** owns the relationship with the client and answers for the promise made
  to them.
- **Booking a task on someone's calendar is assigning it.** A team leader books their team, a
  department lead their department, Traffic anyone. Booking somebody outside your own reach is a
  **request** that person's lead approves or refuses — nothing is booked until they do.
