# ADR-0072: Both brief gates are governed by scope, not by naming roles

- **Status:** accepted (Edward, 5 Sep 2026)
- **Date:** 2026-09-05
- **Open Decision:** —
- **Task(s):** `I40`. Extends [ADR-0070](0070-a-department-brief-is-approved-by-the-department.md)
  (who approves a department brief) and [ADR-0048](0048-briefs-are-generated-per-discipline-and-approved-before-work-starts.md)
  d.8/d.9 (the campaign approval gate); relates to ADR-0067 (draft from step 1), ADR-0008
  (grant-driven surfaces).

## Context

Edward, 5 September 2026: *"Department level briefs are no longer generated by the Account Manager,
the department is responsible for this."* And on ordering, describing what step 5 must say:
*"Review the Campaign Brief and Deliverables and edit if required. Once approved the assigned users
will be notified to complete and approve their briefs."*

ADR-0070 settled who **approves** a department brief and, through migration
`20260903130100`, who is **notified** when one is waiting. Neither that ADR nor any other says who
may **generate** one, or when. That gap is not theoretical — the two halves currently disagree.

### What is already built, and is not in question here

- `app.approve_campaign` inserts `brief_awaiting_approval` for every `department_lead`,
  `team_lead` and `deputy_team_lead` in each department that has an unapproved brief, excluding
  Power Admin and Traffic Manager deliberately (they can approve anything and would otherwise
  receive every brief in the agency). Email copy exists in `notify-email`; in-app copy exists in
  EN and AR.
- `briefs:approve` is seeded to `department_lead`, `team_lead`, `deputy_team_lead`,
  `account_manager`, `power_admin` and `scheduler`. The account manager already holds it.
- There is deliberately **no `owner_user_id` on `campaign_briefs`**. A brief's people are resolved
  by scope — department plus role — not by a named owner.

None of that changes.

### The flaw

`generate-brief` asks one question — `hasProjectsManage(authHeader)` — and it is the wrong shape.
It is a tenant-wide yes/no: *do you hold this grant anywhere?* It never asks **for whose department**.

**A correction, recorded because it was nearly built on.** A first draft of this ADR claimed
department leads cannot generate a brief at all, citing `20260816090000`'s comment that
*"department/team-scoped roles don't hold `projects:manage`"*. **That comment is stale.** The grant
seed later the same day (`20260816110000:90`) copies every Scheduler grant to the Department Lead,
and Scheduler holds `projects:manage` — so department leads have held it since 16 August. The
missing-grant problem does not exist; only the missing scope does. A stale comment was read as
current, and a decision was drafted on it.

So the real defect is narrower and worse:

- **`projects:manage` is not scoped at all here.** Anyone holding it can generate *any* department's
  brief on *any* campaign. "The department is responsible for this" and "whoever holds the grant may
  do it to anyone" are the same code path, so there is no normal route for a fallback to fall back
  *from* — and the audit signal ADR-0070 depends on (*"if Traffic is approving most department
  briefs, that is the signal"*) cannot exist, because acting for your own department and acting for
  someone else's are indistinguishable.
- **Nothing checks campaign approval.** `generate-brief` can be called against a `draft` campaign,
  producing department briefs that describe deliverables the account manager may still delete, for
  a campaign nobody has been told about. Edward set this ordering explicitly; the server does not
  enforce it.

And it has never been caught, because `useGenerateDepartmentBriefs` in
`src/features/campaigns/api/department-briefs-api.ts` **is called from nowhere in the product**.
The hook and its edge function have existed since 27 August with no call site, so the gate has
never been exercised by a real user.

### Options weighed

**Add a new `briefs:generate` grant.** Rejected as a distinction without a difference. Generating a
brief and approving it are the same responsibility held by the same people; a second grant would be
seeded to the same roles and would only add a way for the two to drift apart.

**Move generation to `briefs:approve`.** Considered and dropped. It would work, but it treats a
scoping problem as a grant problem: the holders of `projects:manage` are already the right people,
and swapping the grant would leave the same unscoped check behind it.

**Scope the check.** Chosen. The caller must hold the grant **and** pass `app.in_write_scope` — the
existing primitive (ADR-0037), which already resolves a department lead's department, a team lead's
team within it, an account manager's accounts, and the ADR-0036 per-user overlay. Scoping is what
was missing; the grant never was. Edward, 5 Sep: *"scope permissions on projects:manage… everyone
else's project manage is restricted to their department."* The reach of that rule, and the role that
sits above it, are **ADR-0073**.

## Decision

**d.1 — The grant stays `projects:manage`; what changes is that it is scoped.** Department leads
have held it since 16 August, so nothing needs granting and no role gains authority it did not have.
The first draft of this ADR moved generation to `briefs:approve` on a false premise and is corrected
here rather than quietly.

**d.2 — Generation is scoped by `app.in_write_scope`, and by nothing else.** The caller must hold
`projects:manage` **and** pass `app.in_write_scope` for that department — the same two conditions
ADR-0070 d.3 puts on approving it. **No role is named here and none may be.** Account-scoped roles
already pass that primitive for every department, so Power Admin and Traffic Manager keep the
built-in failsafe *because of the scope model, not beside it* — ADR-0070: *"the failsafe falls out
of the scope model rather than being bolted beside it, which is why it cannot drift from it."* A
first draft of this ADR enumerated those roles by hand and was corrected before implementation;
enumerating them is how the two gates drift apart, and the campaign gate above this one is already
living proof (see d.7).

**d.3 — A department brief cannot be generated before its campaign is approved.**
`generate-brief` rejects a campaign whose `status` is not `approved`, with a stated reason. Approval
is what notifies the department that the brief is theirs to write; generating earlier produces a
document nobody has been told to expect, describing deliverables the account manager may still
change.

**d.4 — Generating on someone's behalf is a fallback, not the normal route.** The account manager,
Power Admin and Scheduler retain the ability by d.1/d.2, for absence. The surface states this
plainly rather than presenting it as the default action, and the primary action on a department tab
is the department's own.

**d.5 — The account manager is confirmed as an approver of a department brief.** Already true in the
seed; recorded here because ADR-0070's prose names only the department's own people and the
agency-wide failsafe, and the omission has already caused one wrong reading.

**d.6 — Ordering, stated once so it is not re-derived.** The account manager reviews and approves
the campaign → the department's leads are notified (`brief_awaiting_approval`, already built) →
the department generates, edits, reviews its tasks, and approves to issue → only then are that
department's tasks assignable. The second gate is ADR-0070's; this ADR adds only that generation
sits inside it rather than before it.

**d.7 — The CAMPAIGN gate is brought onto the same primitive.** `app.approve_campaign` predates
ADR-0070 and does its own identity check —
`if v_am is null or v_am is distinct from auth.uid() then raise` — a bare uid comparison that uses
neither a grant nor `app.in_write_scope`. So **a Power Admin, who holds every grant, is refused**,
and ADR-0070's failsafe has nothing to attach to one level up: a campaign whose account manager has
left, or which never had one, can be approved by **nobody**. That is the 29 August deadlock exactly,
unfixed at the layer above the one that was fixed. Approval now requires holding the approval grant
**and** passing `app.in_write_scope` for the campaign, so the account manager keeps it by their
account scope and the failsafe falls out for the same reason it does everywhere else. The
account-manager-only rule is **not** preserved as a special case: ADR-0048 d.9 named the AM as the
approver in a world with no failsafe, and ADR-0070 has since settled that gates get one.

**d.8 — There is no create-without-approving path out of the wizard.** Step 5 IS the approval step
(I40), so a campaign created there and left unapproved has nowhere to be approved once the separate
approval page is deleted. With d.7 in place everyone who can reach the wizard can approve, so the
last button has one label and one behaviour. A campaign with **no** account manager is refused at
step 1, where it is a blank required field, rather than at step 5 where it is a dead end.

## Consequences

**Easier.** The people told to act can act. A department lead opening the notification lands on a
brief they can actually generate, which is the flow ADR-0070 described and could not deliver.
Generation and approval move together, governed by one grant, so widening or narrowing who is
responsible is a single seed change.

**Harder.** `generate-brief` gains a campaign-status read it did not have, so it can now fail for a
reason unrelated to the caller — the UI must say "the campaign is not approved yet" rather than a
permission error. d.2 also means the function must resolve the caller's scope and department, which
today it does not do at all.

**Locked in.** Brief generation is a department act, permanently downstream of campaign approval.
A future "let the account manager pre-generate the briefs so the departments only review them"
would contradict d.3 and needs a new ADR.

**Two migrations are required.** `generate-brief` moves to `briefs:approve` + `app.in_write_scope`
(d.1/d.2), and `app.approve_campaign` is rewritten onto the same primitive (d.7) — reproduced WHOLE
per the house pattern, since a bare `create or replace` resets `proconfig` and drops the ADR-0033
`search_path` pin. No schema change: no new table, no new column, and specifically **no
`owner_user_id`**, which ADR-0070 deliberately does not have.

**Amends ADR-0048 d.9.** The account manager is no longer the ONLY person who can approve a
campaign. They remain the intended one, and approvals still record who gave them — the guard against
a failsafe becoming a habit is the audit trail, exactly as ADR-0070 argues for the gate below.

**Not addressed here.** Whether a generated brief should be regenerable after approval, and what
happens to concepts proposed against a brief that is regenerated. Both are ADR-0065 territory and
neither blocks I40.
