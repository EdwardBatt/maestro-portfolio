# ADR-0090: The owner approves the task list before anyone is assigned

- **Status:** accepted (Edward, 11 Sep)
- **Date:** 2026-09-11
- **Open Decision:** none — completes the single-job flow (ADR-0082–0086)
- **Task(s):** I44 (the step after SJ-04)

## Context

**Edward's addition to the flow, 10 Sep:** *"Once this is approved and released to the Deliverables
Owner, there should be one additional step for the Deliverables owner and that is review, edit and
approve the autogenerated task list before approving for task assignment."* ADR-0085 d.4 promised
it — *"tasks are generated at release and HELD, not assigned"* — and left the mechanism to this ADR.

**What "generated" already means.** `app.tg_deliverable_materialise_workflow` fires **on insert**
of any deliverable with a type, and `app.materialise_deliverable_workflow` lays down the type's
workflow steps as unassigned `todo` tasks, quantity multiplying the creation step (ADR-0052,
ADR-0071). Measured on the seed: *Social statics ×6* becomes nine tasks — Creative Direction,
Develop Concept, Write Copy, and six *Create Artwork*. So the list exists from the moment SJ-02
writes the deliverable, before anyone has approved anything. **"Held" therefore needs no new
status.** An unassigned `todo` task is already held: it consumes no capacity, sits on nobody's My
Work, and appears on the department tab as planned work nobody has picked up (ADR-0052 d.5's
"unassigned" counts exist for exactly this).

**What "review, edit" already means.** The deliverable detail on `/deliverables` and the campaign
page's department tab carry every edit the owner needs: add a task, remove one (deleted if never
assigned, cancelled otherwise — ADR-0052 d.12/13), retitle and re-date in place (I19.9), reorder
(`resequence_deliverable_tasks`, ADR-0052 d.14), set the review mode (ADR-0059 d.4), appoint
reviewers, declare what a task is waiting for (ADR-0069). Nothing on that list is new.

**A campaign's tasks are already gated TWICE, and the second gate is the department's.**
`app.tg_sub_task_assignment_gate` — the BEFORE trigger every assignment passes, migrations and
system operations exempt — was written for ADR-0048 d.8 (`20260827210000`: no assignment on a
campaign the account manager has not approved) and **redefined by ADR-0070 d.4**
(`20260903130000`): *"the campaign being approved is no longer enough — the department that will
do this work has to have accepted its own brief."* `app.sub_task_brief_is_approved` refuses until
the department has generated (ADR-0072) and approved (ADR-0070) its brief. Edward, 11 Sep,
correcting a first draft of this ADR that had read only the earlier definition: *"after the
campaign is approved, the department owners are required to generate and confirm department level
briefs and tasks for deliverables assigned and only once approved do they surface for selection."*
That is what is built. The first draft's claim that *"the AM's approval opens everything"* was
wrong, and it was wrong for the reason `traps.md` records under *reproducing a function from the
wrong base* — the trigger was read from its first migration, not its last.

**For a single job, that second gate is passed by the AM, so nothing department-side remains.**
ADR-0084 d.4: the single-execution brief is approved WITH the job, by the account manager, on
SJ-04 — there is no department brief to generate and no department approval, by Edward's decision.
So `sub_task_brief_is_approved` is true the moment the job is released, and a single job's tasks
would be assignable at once with nobody who owns the work having said *"this is the list."*
**That is the gap, and it is a single-job gap.** Edward's step — the owner reviews, edits and
approves the generated list — is the single job's equivalent of the campaign's department
acceptance. Not a third gate; the second one, in the shape a single job needs.

**Who owns the deliverable.** ADR-0083 d.8/d.9: the department lead named on SJ-02, reassignable
to one of their team leads, never a team member. `deliverables.owner_user_id`. And
`deliverable_owner:manage` (ADR-0055) is the grant for changing who that is.

**The reminder from SJ-03.** ADR-0084 d.8 kept *"what could stop this"* as free text precisely so
the owner would see it here and attach the real dependency to the right task. This is where it
surfaces.

## Decision

1. **Tasks are generated where they are today — on deliverable insert — and are held by being
   unassigned.** No new task status, no "proposed" state. The list the owner reviews is the list
   the trigger wrote, editable with the tools that exist.

2. **A deliverable gains `tasks_approved_at` and `tasks_approved_by`**, null until the owner
   approves the list. Two columns, server-written only, the same shape as every other approval
   stamp in this schema (`approved_at`/`approved_by` on campaigns, on briefs, on tasks).

3. **Assignment is refused while `tasks_approved_at` is null**, in `app.tg_sub_task_assignment_gate`,
   as a second check after the campaign-approval one it sits beside, with its own message that names
   the deliverable and its owner: *"this task list has not been approved by <owner> yet."*
   Migrations and system operations pass, as they do for the existing check. **This is the whole
   mechanism**: one column, one condition, in the one place assignment already has to pass.

3a. **A held task is not offered anywhere assignment starts.** Edward, 11 Sep, confirming the
   reading: *"until the task list is approved it should not be visible from /schedule and cannot be
   assigned."* On the grid it is invisible by construction — a task appears on a person's row only
   once it is booked, and a held task has no booking. The `+` picker (ADR-0082 d.8, which lists a
   project's tasks to assign) **excludes tasks whose deliverable has `tasks_approved_at` null** —
   absence, not a disabled entry (design doc §2.3, the rule `nav-config` follows), because offering
   a task d.3 will refuse is a dead end. The same filter applies to bulk assignment when `I39`
   builds it.

3b. **Unassigned work is the OWNER's to assign; the Traffic Manager is the backstop.** Edward,
   11 Sep: *"Unassigned tasks should surface for the manager, but it is the deliverables owners who
   should assign, the traffic manager is the backstop."* So the unassigned counts and panels —
   `UnassignedWork` on the dashboard, the campaign page's rollup (I40.21), `unassignedCount` on the
   deliverables list — surface to BOTH, and the owner is the one expected to act; Traffic sees the
   same list to step in where an owner has not. (`exceptions:view` is already seeded to department
   and team leads as well as to Traffic and the AM, so the surfaces reach the right people today.)
   **A held task is a different line on that list.** It is unassigned, but it is not yet work
   anybody should assign — the owner has not approved the list — so those surfaces count and label
   it as **awaiting the owner's review**, naming the owner, separately from work that is ready to
   assign. For the owner that line reads *approve your list*; for Traffic it reads as a backstop
   signal, and Traffic can act on it — d.4 lets anyone with `deliverable_owner:manage` in scope
   approve a list for an absent owner, and Traffic's tenant scope covers every deliverable. The
   number is always visible: a list sitting unapproved for a week is a real exception.

4. **The owner approves the list through `public.approve_deliverable_tasks(p_deliverable)`.**
   Authority: the deliverable's `owner_user_id`, or anyone holding `deliverable_owner:manage` within
   `in_write_scope` of the deliverable — the person who could have changed the owner may act for an
   absent one. Computed from the row and from grants, never from a role name (ADR-0073/0074). Stamps
   d.2. Idempotent: approving an approved list changes nothing.

5. **The list may still be edited after approval, and edits are ordinary edits.** Approval is a
   one-time gate that opens assignment; it is not a lock. A task added afterwards is assignable at
   once, because the person adding it is the owner or someone with authority over the list, and the
   audit trigger records it. A second approval ceremony every time a task is retitled would be the
   friction that teaches people to approve without reading. If a type change re-materialises the
   list (`tg_deliverable_materialise_workflow` on `deliverable_type_id`), the new tasks are likewise
   assignable — the owner changed the type.

6. **Single jobs only. Campaigns keep the gate they have.** A campaign deliverable's work is
   accepted by its department approving its brief (ADR-0070 d.4); a single job's is accepted by its
   owner approving its task list. **One question, two answers by kind of job** — and it is written
   that way rather than as a `kind` branch: the second check in `tg_sub_task_assignment_gate`
   becomes *"has the department accepted this work?"*, answered by `sub_task_brief_is_approved`
   where the campaign has department briefs, and by `tasks_approved_at` where it does not (a single
   job has none, by ADR-0084 d.3). The first draft of this decision extended the task-list approval
   to campaigns as a third gate; that was withdrawn once the second gate was found. **No backfill is
   needed**: every existing deliverable is on a campaign with department briefs, and its gate is the
   one it already has. **Nothing about the campaign flow the pilot runs today changes.**

7. **The review happens where the list already lives — the deliverable detail — with one added
   action.** No new screen. The detail, opened from the owner's queue or the campaign tab, shows the
   generated list with its editing tools and an **Approve task list** button while d.2 is null; once
   stamped, the button becomes the stamp (*approved by Fadia, 11 Sep*). Above the list, **the
   "what could stop this" text from SJ-03** (ADR-0084 d.8), shown until every task has been given a
   dependency or the text is dismissed by the owner — a reminder, not a record.

8. **The owner is told at release (ADR-0085 d.4) and nobody is told again at approval.** Approving
   the list is the owner's own act, and the person who assigns next is almost always the same
   person. A `task_list_approved` notification was weighed and not built: it would tell the
   approver what they just did. What IS visible is the stamp on the deliverable row, on the
   department tab and on `/deliverables`, so a Traffic Manager assigning on the department's behalf
   can see the list is ready. If the pilot shows Traffic waiting on a signal, that is a notification
   type to add, not a rule to change.

9. **Not approving is not a state.** A deliverable whose list is unapproved is in `in_progress` like
   any other; nothing else about it waits. The campaign's own progress rollup (I40.21) counts its
   tasks as *unassigned*, which is what they are, and ADR-0075's at-risk arithmetic treats not-started
   work as it always has — an unapproved list on a job due in three days is at risk, and should say so.

## Consequences

**Easier.** The whole feature is two columns, one condition in a trigger that already exists, one
RPC, and one button on a screen that already has every editing tool. The owner's step is real —
nothing can be assigned around it — without a new status, a new screen, or a new notification.
The "what could stop this" hand-off (0084 d.8) gets the place it was promised.

**Harder.** Two kinds of job now answer "has the department accepted this?" two ways, and anyone
reading `tg_sub_task_assignment_gate` has to hold both in their head — the ADR says why, but the
trigger has to say it too, in its comment. And a deliverable with no owner has nobody to approve its list — d.4's failsafe covers it, but
`owner_user_id` is nullable and an ownerless deliverable is now a deliverable whose work cannot be
assigned by anyone below `deliverable_owner:manage`, which is a stronger consequence of leaving the
owner blank than exists today.

**Locked in.** That a generated list is a proposal until its owner says otherwise, and the system
enforces the difference at the one door assignment goes through. That "held" is unassigned — no
fourth task state. And that the owner's approval is a gate, not a lock: the list keeps being theirs
to change.

## Addendum — 11 Sep, from the build

**d.6 could not be built as written, and the trigger branches on `campaigns.kind` after all.** The
decision said the second gate would ask *"has the department accepted this work?"* and answer it by
`sub_task_brief_is_approved` *where the campaign has department briefs*. But ADR-0084 d.3 stores a
single job's brief in `campaign_briefs` with a department too — so that test is true for single jobs
as well, and the alternative reading (a department row exists but was not generated by the
department) would have changed campaign behaviour. The builder branched on `kind`, which d.6 had
called the worse shape; it is the honest one, and the trigger's comment says why
(`20260911130100`). One edge follows: a promoted job's original deliverable becomes assignable through
its already-approved brief, which is correct — that deliverable's owner approved its list before
promotion or the job could not have been released.

## Not decided here

Whether the campaign wizard should show the generated lists at step 5 for the account manager to
see before approving (it shows none today — ADR-0052 materialises at submit, and the wizard's
"Review & Approve" reviews the brief and deliverables, not tasks). Bulk assignment across an
approved list (`I39`). Whether a Traffic Manager needs to be told when a list is approved (d.8).

## Addendum — 12 Sep, the account manager does not approve the task list

**The account manager was able to approve the task list; that is reversed.** d.4 admitted "anyone
holding `deliverable_owner:manage` within scope of the deliverable". Migration `20260911130600`, on
review, passed the deliverable's account manager into `app.in_write_scope` (it had been passing
NULL, which the reviewer read as a null-argument bug) — so the campaign's AM could approve the task
list on their own job through the ACCOUNT branch. Edward, testing 12 Sep: *"the AM can approve the
project/campaign… they cannot approve the task list… The Dept Manager owns the deliverable and can
assign it to one of his team leads."*

**Decided.** The account manager's authority ends at approving the **campaign** (ADR-0048 d.8). The
task list belongs to the **Department Manager** who owns the deliverable: they approve it and assign
it to one of their team leads. We are enforcing accountability where the responsibility sits — the
person who signs off the list is the person who owns delivering it.

- **d.4, amended.** Authority to approve a task list is: the **owner** of record, **or**
  `deliverable_owner:manage` within the deliverable's **department** scope (a lead in that
  department; and Power Admin / Traffic Manager by tenant scope — the **failsafe for an absent
  owner, which stays**). The **account branch is no longer offered** to this gate: the AM is
  refused. The AM keeps `deliverable_owner:manage` for reassigning a deliverable's owner on their
  campaign (ADR-0055 / `20260828120000`) — that is unchanged; it simply no longer reaches task-list
  approval.
- **One predicate.** The authority is extracted into `app.may_approve_deliverable_tasks(deliverables)`;
  `approve_deliverable_tasks` raises on it, and a PostgREST computed column
  `public.can_approve_tasks(deliverables)` exposes the same answer so the UI offers the button to
  exactly whom the RPC admits. The "Approve task list" button had been gated on an **unscoped**
  `can('deliverable_owner:manage')` check, which offered it to holders the RPC would refuse (a
  different AM, a cross-department lead) and dead-ended them — fixed here.

Migration `20260912110000`. pgTAP `103` now asserts the AM is refused and that `can_approve_tasks`
agrees with the RPC. **ADR-0055 d.3b is narrowed accordingly** for this one gate.

## Addendum — 20 Sep, d.3a applies to campaigns too, and the picker asks the gate's own function

d.3a was built for single jobs only: the `/schedule` picker left out a deliverable with
`tasks_approved_at` null when the campaign's `kind` was `single_job`, in the client, and offered
every deliverable of a campaign — the department-brief gate (d.6) being left to refuse on submit.
Edward, 20 Sep, on a campaign with no briefs: *"you are making deliverables available for assignment
on the schedule when the tasks have not been approved."*

**Now.** `public.assignable_deliverables(p_campaign)` — security invoker, so RLS on `deliverables`
applies — returns the campaign's live deliverables for which `app.deliverable_work_acceptance_refusal`
is null and the campaign is approved: the one question of d.6, asked of the deliverable rather than
the task, from the same function `tg_sub_task_assignment_gate` reads. The picker reads that and
carries no rule of its own, so it and the trigger cannot disagree. A held deliverable is absent, not
disabled, for every kind of job. With ADR-0070's 20 Sep addendum a campaign department that has not
generated its brief is held too. Migration `20260920150000`; pgTAP 133.
