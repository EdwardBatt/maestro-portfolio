# ADR-0085: The account manager releases a single job in one press

- **Status:** accepted (Edward, 11 Sep)
- **Date:** 2026-09-11
- **Open Decision:** none
- **Task(s):** I44 (SJ-04)

## Context

**SJ-04 is the last screen before a job reaches a team.** The mock lays out everything captured on
SJ-02 and SJ-03 as it will land — the title, type and due date; the client's own words; who asked
and when; the brief fields; references, dependencies and attachments; the proof — under the running
response time, and then says where it goes: *"Creative team. Fadia is the lead. It lands in her
queue the moment you open the job. She picks who does it."*

**Edward has already decided the three things that matter on this screen**, across 10–11 Sep, and
this ADR records them rather than re-asking:

- *"SJ-04 should be the review and approve screen."*
- *"For this flow, the AM approves it for release."*
- *"Probably the approve button triggers this check"* — the duplicate check, SJ-A1.
- *"Once this is approved and released to the Deliverables Owner, there should be one additional
  step for the Deliverables owner and that is review, edit and approve the autogenerated task list
  before approving for task assignment."*

**Two things the mock puts on this screen belong elsewhere or nowhere.** The *"Turns out to be bigger
than this? Promote to campaign"* box: Edward moved promotion to an action on the campaigns list
(SJ-A2), not here. And *"What Ahmed sees"* — the client-facing view of the job — describes the
client portal, which is `M10` and not built; a panel explaining a view that does not exist would be
a promise the screen cannot keep.

**What exists.** `public.approve_campaign` (ADR-0073, scoped) — the press that turns
`pending_approval` into `approved`, with `approved_by`/`approved_at` stamped and authority checked
by grant and scope, never by role name. `campaign_briefs.approved_at`/`approved_by` (ADR-0070). The
audit trigger on every tenant-scoped table (AGENTS.md §6), so *"after you open the job, changes are
tracked"* is already true of everything. `app.materialise_deliverable_workflow`, which generates a
deliverable's tasks from its workflow template — today from a trigger, the instant the deliverable
is written. Campaign drafts (ADR-0067), resumable, so a wizard abandoned at step four is not lost.

## Decision

1. **SJ-04 is read-only review, and its one action is Approve.** Everything from SJ-02 and SJ-03
   shown as it will land, nothing editable in place — Back to change it. Two buttons: **Approve**
   and **Cancel**. The mock's *"Open the job"* is renamed, because Edward named the screen for what
   the press does.

2. **Approve is one press by the account manager, and it does three things as one act:** the job's
   status becomes `approved` (through `public.approve_campaign`, the existing scoped RPC — a single
   job is a campaign row, ADR-0083 d.3); its brief is stamped approved (ADR-0084 d.4, no second
   gate); and the deliverable is **released to its owner** — the department lead named on SJ-02.
   Authority is `approve_campaign`'s own: grant plus scope, ADR-0073, unchanged.

3. **The duplicate check fires on Approve, before any of d.2 happens.** Edward: *"probably the
   approve button triggers this check."* If an open job for the same client has a similar name,
   SJ-A1 appears over this screen and the approval waits on its answer — add this as a deliverable on
   that job, or keep them separate. **Only on Approve**, not on every keystroke of the title: a check
   that interrupts typing teaches people to dismiss it. What "similar" means and what attaching does
   to the clock is SJ-A1's ADR; this decision is only *when*.

4. **"Released to the owner" means the job enters the owner's queue at the task-list review step,
   and they are told.** The deliverable is already theirs (ADR-0083 d.8); the release is the moment
   it becomes actionable. The lead receives a notification — the existing `assigned` type, the row
   being what it says — and the job appears in their work with its generated task list waiting for
   review. **Tasks are generated at release and HELD**, not assigned: the owner reviews, edits and
   approves the list before anyone is assigned anything. That step, and how "held" is represented,
   is its own ADR; this decision fixes only that release produces the list and hands it over.

5. **The response time is shown, running from the arrival confirmed on SJ-02.** Not from the moment
   the form was opened. The number is the gap between the client asking and the job being released,
   and this is the screen where that gap ends — so it is the one place the number should be
   prominent rather than a footnote.

6. **No promote box.** Promotion is an action on the campaigns list (SJ-A2, Edward, 10 Sep). A job
   that turns out to be bigger is promoted after it exists, from where jobs are listed, not guessed at
   before it has been released.

7. **No "what the client sees" panel.** The client portal is `M10` and not built. When it is, the
   mock's sentence is the right spec for it — *"the due date, the review point, and that it is in
   hand; never the department, never who is drawing it, never hours"* — and it is recorded here so
   that ADR can pick it up. Until then the screen does not describe it.

8. **Cancel leaves a draft, exactly as the campaign wizard does (ADR-0067).** The job already
   exists as a draft campaign row from SJ-02 — it has to, because the receipt and the attachments
   were filed into its folder — so Cancel cannot mean "nothing happened". It means "not released":
   the draft is resumable from the campaigns list or deleted from there, the same two choices a
   half-finished campaign has. **A draft is never released by accident**: nothing but the Approve
   press on this screen changes its status.

9. **After release, edits are tracked and the owner sees them.** The mock's *"changes are tracked
   and Fadia sees them"* is already true — the audit trigger records every change on every table
   this touches — and this decision only says the screen may claim it. What the owner is *shown* of
   a post-release edit is the campaign page's concern, not this screen's.

## Consequences

**Easier.** The release is one press calling one RPC that exists, with authority already scoped and
already tested per actor (`087`, `090`). Drafts, audit and notification all come from what is built.
The account manager reads one screen and presses one button, and the number that matters — how long
the client has been waiting — is in front of them when they do.

**Harder.** d.4 changes when tasks become real: today they materialise the instant a deliverable is
written, from a trigger; now they are generated at release and held for review, which is a new state
between "exists" and "assignable" and the subject of the next ADR. Until that ADR lands, d.4 is a
promise about ordering, not a mechanism. And d.3 makes Approve the screen where the duplicate check
lives, so if the check is slow or noisy it is the release that suffers — the matching rule (SJ-A1)
has to be cheap and quiet or people will learn to press through it.

**Locked in.** That releasing a single job is one act by one person, not a sequence. That a job's
owner is told and handed a list to review, never a set of tasks already assigned. That promotion
happens to a job that exists, from the list, not on the way in. And that this screen does not
describe what a client will see until a client can see anything.

## Addendum — 11 Sep, from the build and the review

**d.4's notification is `campaign_approved`, not `assigned`.** The build reasoned it in the
migration header (`20260911130200`): the owner is handed a job, not a task, and `assigned` names a
task on My Work. The review asked for the ADR to say so; it does now.

**A duplicate check that cannot run is not a refusal.** d.3 says the check warns and never
refuses. The first build returned from Approve when `similar_open_jobs` threw — which made every
release depend on `pg_trgm` answering. Now the release goes ahead and the person is told once,
before the job opens, that the check did not happen.

**Due dates land at the close of the working day.** The typed day (ADR-0083 d.10) is written as
18:00 Iraq (ADR-0076 d.3), the way `/deliverables` writes one; the first build wrote the bare day,
which is midnight UTC.
